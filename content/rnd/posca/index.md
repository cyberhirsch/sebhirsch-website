### The Study

*Posca* was the everyday drink of the Roman legions: sour wine cut with water,
cheap, and profoundly unglamorous. I built it a packshot anyway.

The conceit is not a joke, or not only a joke. It sets up an interesting collision:
the visual grammar of contemporary beverage advertising — the hero bottle, the
shallow depth of field, the warm rim separating the product from a softly dying
background — applied to an object that has no brand, no marketing department, and
two thousand years of distance from the entire apparatus. What survives the
transplant tells you something about how much of that grammar is genuinely about
selling and how much is simply how we have learned to *look* at objects.

### What It Was Actually For

Underneath the concept, this was a materials-and-light exercise, and I chose the
set to make it a demanding one. Almost every difficult surface class is present in
one frame:

- **Glass with a coloured liquid inside it** — refraction, absorption through
  depth, the meniscus, and the caustic the whole assembly throws onto the wood.
- **Worn end-grain timber** — a tabletop that has to read as scratched, oiled and
  handled rather than as a wood texture on a plane.
- **Unglazed terracotta and clay** — near-diffuse, subtly porous, the exact opposite
  response to light of everything around it.
- **Tarnished silver coins and a polished blade** — anisotropic metal that picks up
  and distorts the whole room, sitting directly beside the matte surfaces so the
  contrast is unavoidable.

The lighting is entirely practical and motivated: warm lamps falling off into
depth, a barrel and tavern furniture dissolving into bokeh, and a single cool
accent at the frame edge to keep the whole thing from collapsing into one amber
mass. Getting a warm-on-warm image to hold separation without introducing a light
that could not exist in the room is the real difficulty here, and it is a
constraint I still argue for: motivated light forces better decisions than free
light does.

A deep dive into realistic materials and lighting, dressed as a commercial.
